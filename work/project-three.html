Redesigning onboarding to cut time‑to‑value

One-sentence summary of the outcome, with a number in it if you have one: “New users reached their first success [X]% faster, and activation rose from [A]% to [B]%.”

Role
Lead product designer
Team
1 PM, 3 engineers, 1 researcher
Timeline
10 weeks, Q1 2025
Scope
Research, IA, interaction, visual, QA

The problem

Set the scene in 2–3 sentences. What was broken, for whom, and how you knew (a metric, a support theme, sales feedback). Resist jumping to the solution.

Chart or screenshot of the “before” state

What I did

Walk through the process as a story, not a checklist. A few beats that usually land well:

  • Framing: how you defined success and what you deliberately left out.
  • Research: what you did, the one or two insights that changed the direction.
  • Exploration: the approaches you weighed — show a discarded one and why.
  • Decisions: the hard trade-off you made and the reasoning behind it.
Optional pull quote: a user reaction, a teammate’s comment, or a principle you set.

Outcome

The results. Lead with impact (metrics, adoption, what shipped), then what you learned and what you’d do differently. Honesty here reads as senior.

  • Activation: [A]% → [B]% over [timeframe]
  • Time to first value: down [X]%
  • Shipped to 100% of new users on [date]; now the default flow
